# ADR 0035: 4-bit weights with round-to-nearest are not an extension of int8

Status: study, recorded. No 4-bit format or kernel is added. See
spec/measurements/cuda-rtx4060-int4-study.json.

## Context

ADR 0034 added int8 weights with one scale per row. The changes stayed
small: 96.3% top-1 agreement and +0.6% perplexity. Would 4 bits be the
same idea with half the bytes again? The question is about rounding error
first, so it was measured before writing any 4-bit kernel.

## Method

`gpu_int4_study` (src/device/tests.rs, ignored) fake-quantizes the model
and compares it with BF16 on the GPU:

- **Rounding.** Every projection is rounded to a grid and back to f32.
  This includes the vocabulary projection, as in ADR 0034. The
  embedding lookup, conv weights and vectors keep their values.
- **Running.** The rounded matrices run through the FP32 weight path (cuBLAS
  on CUDA). So only the rounding differs from BF16, not the kernels.
- **Grids** (`Grid` in the test). All use round-half-even.
  - `sym`: scale = max |w| / (2^(b−1) − 1).
  - `affine`: scale = (max − min) / (2^b − 1), with an offset min.
  - Each takes one scale per row or per group of 128, 64 or 32 values.
- **Size.** Bits per weight count the values plus FP16 scales, and FP16
  minimums for affine. The study itself keeps the scales in FP32.
- **Measures.** As `device_real_int8_quality`: README.md, a 32-token
  prompt, then 512 positions fed token by token. At every position: top-1
  agreement, the rank of the BF16 top-1, KL(BF16 ‖ q), the largest logit
  difference, and the perplexity of the text. The residual after every
  layer is captured at positions 31 and 542.
- **Validation.** The unrounded control agrees exactly. The `int8 sym row`
  variant reproduces the real int8 kernels of ADR 0034 to the reported
  digits (96.3%, KL 0.0024, perplexity 25.57).

## Results (BF16 perplexity 25.41)

| grid | bits/weight | top-1 | KL mean | perplexity | BF16 top-1 rank, max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| int8 sym row | 8.0 | 96.3% | 0.002 | 25.57 (+0.6%) | 1 |
| int4 sym row | 4.0 | 45.5% | 1.15 | 71.92 (+183%) | 502 |
| int4 sym g128 | 4.1 | 67.4% | 0.41 | 39.92 (+57%) | 52 |
| int4 sym g64 | 4.3 | 69.7% | 0.30 | 35.91 (+41%) | 25 |
| int4 sym g32 | 4.5 | 74.0% | 0.23 | 32.29 (+27%) | 15 |
| int4 affine g64 | 4.5 | 76.2% | 0.18 | 30.53 (+20%) | 10 |
| int4 affine g32 | 5.0 | 81.8% | 0.13 | 27.59 (+8.6%) | 14 |
| int4 sym g64, vocabulary projection BF16 | 4.3 | 71.7% | 0.27 | 33.83 (+33%) | 19 |

The relative L2 difference of the residual after each layer is the mean
over the 24 layers:

| grid | position 31 | position 542 |
|---|---|---|
| int8 sym row | 2.3% | 2.9% |
| int4 sym row | 49% | 62% |
| int4 sym g64 | 26% | 34% (peak 49% at layer 11) |
| int4 affine g64 | 24% | 25% |

What the measurements show:

1. **One scale per row does not work at 4 bits.** Seven levels per sign
   leave most values of a row at 0 or ±1 next to its largest value. The
   error bound (scale / 2) is about 18 times that of int8. Perplexity
   nearly triples. Group-wise scales are a precondition, not a refinement.
2. **At the same size, affine beats symmetric.** At 4.5 bits per weight,
   affine g64 gives +20% and sym g32 gives +27%.
3. **Round-to-nearest 4-bit stays far from int8.** The best grid tried
   (affine g32, 5.0 bits) is +8.6%, against +0.6%. For this 0.8B model,
   4 bits needs rounding chosen with data (GPTQ, AWQ), not only finer
   scales.
4. **Most of the loss is in the layers, not the vocabulary projection.**
   Keeping that projection in BF16 improves sym g64 only from +41% to
   +33%.
5. **The 4-bit residual errors are larger at the later position.** With
   sym g64 they peak in the middle layers there. Whether errors build up
   in the DeltaNet state over positions cannot be told from two
   positions. It would need an error curve over positions.

## Decision

No 4-bit format with round-to-nearest. The study and its test stay. The
next step is rounding with data (GPTQ). Its result decides whether a
4-bit format and kernels are worth adding.

## Limits

One text (README.md, 512 positions). Rounding error only: no 4-bit
kernels, so no speed. FP32 scales in the study.
