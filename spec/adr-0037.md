# ADR 0037: Training below the last layer: a CPU backward through the whole stack

Status: accepted; implemented (PRs #10–#14), step 6 measured. Results:

- Attention and DeltaNet blocks equal the inference path on the real weights
  (layers 23 and 22: max abs 5.5e-6 and 1.2e-6).
- The backward matches numerical differentiation and PyTorch autograd on the
  synthetic model (every element within 2e-5 + 2e-4|e|).
- On the real weights, from layer 15 with the FFNs of 15 and 19 trainable
  (`tools/real_backward.py`, 56 tokens), the gradient toward the input is
  within 5.9e-5 relative of autograd's, and the FFN gradients within 7.8e-4
  (norms and five whole rows of each matrix). The directional check was not
  added: autograd on the same weights is a stronger check.
- Time on the CPU, 1,024 positions: an attention block 0.22 s forward +
  0.32 s backward, a DeltaNet block 0.32 + 0.62 s, and layers 15–23 with 256
  scored positions 6.9 s.
- The experiment (step 6, `spec/measurements/rocm-thinking-wait-stack.json`):
  KL distillation of the ch5 edit into the FFNs of layers 15 and 19 closes
  10 of the held-out 20 (362 Wait, 6 short loops), against 15/316/2 for the
  edit and 12/376/5 for layer 23 alone. Training the edit's own layers did
  not do better, at one seed. The trained change of `down_proj` is not the
  edit (cosine 0.05 and 0.04, not concentrated in its 64 columns), but
  8.8% and 7.9% of it lies along the edit's output direction (random 0.1%),
  and its top singular directions have |cos| 0.43–0.46 with it: the same
  direction, written from many units. The CE control (the text's IDs, same
  layers and settings) closes 12/313/2, but only 0.5% and 0.4% of its change
  lies along the edit's direction: CE fails only at layer 23, and the
  direction comes from the teacher's distribution. Seeds 1 and 2 (added
  2026-11-24) repeat both: KL at layers 15/19 closes 10, 12, 13 over seeds
  0–2, CE 12, 12, 14, and KL at layer 23 12, 13, 11, so neither the layer
  choice nor KL versus CE separates in the count; CE keeps 2 short loops at
  every seed. The weight result holds at every seed: KL's change lies 8–9%
  along the edit's direction (top singular |cos| 0.42–0.47), CE's 0.4–0.5%.
  A step of 4 sequences × 256 scored positions through layers 15–23 takes
  about 24 s.

## Context

The book 3 training code (`src/train/`, feature `train`) can only change
the last layer's FFN (layer 23). Every layer below it is frozen, and what
layer 23's FFN reads is computed once and cached (`cache`, `corpus`, GPU
capture). The only backward passes are the FFN, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, the head
and the losses (`grad`, `ffn`, `head`).

The experiments so far point below layer 23:

- The probes (cpu-prefecture-capitals-probe.json) find the prefecture after
  layers 15 and 19.
- The volume 1 ch5 edit (Weight-Space Programming) subtracts one direction
  from the FFN `down_proj` of layers 15 and 19.
- KL distillation of that edit into layer 23 alone gets 12 of 20 closed,
  against the edit's 15 (rocm-thinking-wait-training.json).

To train those layers, the gradient has to pass back through every layer
above them, through attention and Gated DeltaNet. A framework such as
PyTorch would do this, but then the part of the book that explains training
would be code the reader cannot see. The rule of volumes 1 and 2 holds:
our own Rust code computes it. PyTorch may be used freely for the checks, on the synthetic model and on the real weights, but never to compute what training uses.

## Decision

Write the backward of every block on the CPU, f32, as a reference
implementation. Speed is secondary. There is no GPU backward.

### Scope

- **Trainable:** the FFN of any set of layers, either in full or with LoRA.
  LoRA on the mixer projections (q/k/v/o, DeltaNet qkv/z/a/b/out) comes
  later, once the mixer backward is checked. The embedding, norms and gates
  stay frozen.
- **Gradient path:** from the loss, back through the head, then layers
  23, 22, … down to the lowest trained layer L. Layers below L are frozen,
  and their output (the residual stream entering L) is cached, as layer
  23's inputs are now.
- **Unchanged:** the teacher (the edited model's top K, computed on the
  GPU), the losses, the optimizers, checkpoints, and the evaluation
  (`thinking_survey.py` with `--ffn-checkpoint`, extended to several
  layers).

### Forward for training

- The forward runs a whole sequence at once, layer by layer, from L to 23.
  This is the "sequence" form that `AttentionWeights::sequence` already
  uses. Each layer keeps what its backward needs.
- It is checked against the inference path: same activations, within f32
  tolerance.

### Backward of each block

- **Block:** the residual adds and the two zero-centered RMSNorms. The
  RMSNorm backward exists; add only the weightless path.
- **FFN:** exists (`TrainFfn::backward`).
- **Attention** (layers 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23; 8 heads, 2 KV heads,
  head_dim 256, partial RoPE 64):
  - q/k/v/o projections;
  - per-head zero-centered RMSNorm on q and k;
  - RoPE (a rotation, so its backward is the inverse rotation);
  - causal softmax over scores, with GQA: each KV head sums the gradients
    of the 4 query heads that share it;
  - the sigmoid output gate.
  - Memory: the probabilities are 8 × T × T per layer, 32 MB at T = 1,000.
    They are recomputed in the backward rather than kept.
- **Gated DeltaNet** (the other 18 layers; 16 heads, k = v = 128,
  conv kernel 4):
  - qkv/z/a/b/out projections;
  - the causal depthwise convolution and SiLU;
  - L2 norm of q and k, and the 1/√d scale of q;
  - the gates: β = sigmoid(b), g = −exp(A_log)·softplus(a + dt);
  - the delta rule recurrence;
  - the gated RMSNorm (direct scale) times SiLU(z).
  - **The recurrence is the hard part.** Use the plain token-by-token form
    (`delta_rule`), not the chunked one, and take its backward by stepping
    back through time. Each step needs the state S_{t−1}. That is 16 × 128
    × 128 floats, 1 MB per token per layer, so not every state is kept.
    The state is saved every 64 tokens, and the backward recomputes the 64
    states of a chunk from its saved state. That costs one more forward of
    the recurrence and needs 64 MB per layer during the backward.

### Checks (in this order; no step starts until the one before passes)

1. **Each operation against numerical differentiation**, in f64, in the
   style of `tests/train_grad.rs`. Covers RoPE, L2 norm, causal softmax
   with GQA, the convolution, the gates, the gated RMSNorm, and the delta
   rule over a whole short sequence, including the chunked recomputation
   (the result is the same with and without it).
2. **Each layer against PyTorch autograd.** A new `tools/generate_backward.py`
   runs the pinned upstream classes as `generate_hybrid.py` does
   (`Qwen3_5Attention`, `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet` with
   `torch_recurrent_gated_delta_rule`). It runs them on the small synthetic
   hybrid model, and writes the gradients of a fixed loss with respect to
   the layer input and every weight. The gradients are fixtures, with
   their SHA-256 in tests/fixtures/SHA256SUMS. Torch only gives the
   expected numbers; Rust computes them.
3. **The whole stack on the synthetic model** against PyTorch: the
   gradient of a loss at the head with respect to the FFN of a low layer.
4. **The real model:**
   - At L = 23, the new path gives the same FFN gradient as the existing
     `TopFfn` (within f32 tolerance).
   - For L = 15, against PyTorch autograd on the real weights. A new
     `tools/real_backward.py` loads the BF16 weights into the pinned
     upstream model, on the CPU in f32. For a short prompt (about 64
     tokens) and a fixed loss, it writes the gradient with respect to
     layer 15's and layer 19's FFN, and with respect to the residual
     entering layer 15. The output is a local file, not a committed
     fixture, as with the other real-model checks.
   - Also a directional check, which needs no PyTorch: perturb the FFN
     along a random direction, and compare the change of the loss with
     gradient · direction (central difference, several step sizes).

### Cost (estimate, to be measured)

- One sequence of 1,000 tokens through layers 15–23 is about 0.4 TFLOP
  forward and twice that backward.
- The head is computed only at the scored positions that are sampled (for
  example 256 per sequence), since a full head costs 0.5 GFLOP per position.
- That is roughly 10–20 s per sequence on the 24-thread CPU. 200 steps of 4
  sequences is about 3–4 hours. This is slower than the layer-23 runs
  (7.5 min), but enough for the experiments.
- Positions can no longer be sampled across the corpus as `--sample` does
  now, because a lower layer's output at one position depends on the earlier
  positions. A step is a few whole sequences. The loss can still use a
  sample of their positions.

### Cache of the inputs of layer L

Generalize the layer-23 cache to "the residual stream entering layer L".
The GPU capture (`DeviceRun::set_capture_top_inputs`) gets a layer
argument. The CPU capture remains the reference, and the two are compared
as before.

## Plan (one PR each, merged in order)

1. **Training forward and attention backward.** Sequence form of one block
   with the saved activations, equal to the inference path. The attention
   backward and its numerical checks.
2. **DeltaNet backward.** The convolution, gates, recurrence (with chunked
   recomputation) and gated norm, and their numerical checks.
3. **PyTorch fixtures.** `tools/generate_backward.py`, the gradients of
   both layer types and of a two-block stack on the synthetic model, and
   the tests against them.
4. **Stack.** The cache of layer L (CPU and GPU), forward and backward from
   L to the head, trainable FFNs at any set of layers. Equal to `TopFfn` at
   L = 23; on the real model, PyTorch autograd at layers 15/19 and the directional check.
5. **Command.** `train-text --layers 15,19` (full FFN or `--lora`), steps
   made of whole sequences, checkpoints holding several layers, and
   `--ffn-checkpoint` in the evaluation reading them. Measure the time and
   memory.
6. **Experiment.** KL distillation of the ch5 edit into layers 15/19:
   - closed / Wait / short loops, against the edit and layer 23;
   - the direction the training wrote into `down_proj`, against the edit's
     direction in the same layer (cosine, and through the LM head).

Each PR is self-contained: tests pass, and the earlier commands are
unchanged.

## Not decided here

- LoRA on the mixer projections (after PR 4, if an experiment needs it).
- Any GPU backward.
- Training the embedding or the norms.
