# ADR 0028: the MTP draft on the device (Metal)

Status: accepted. See spec/measurements/metal-m5-speculative-device.json and
ADR 0027 (the same loop with the draft on the CPU).

## Context

With the draft on the CPU, speculative decoding reached 1.40x (ADR 0027).
The CPU draft took 1.9-3.7 ms per verify pass (0.14-0.27 decode steps), and
the GPU sat idle during it, which also made the verify passes slower.

## Decision

- The MTP layer is a one-block device model (`DeviceModel::load_mtp`,
  src/device/speculative.rs). It shares the main model's bridge context.
  Its block is the MTP attention block and its final norm is the MTP norm.
  Its "embedding", used only as the output projection, is the draft
  subset's rows (`DraftVocab`, ADR 0025), and its vocabulary size is the
  subset size. `fc` and the two input norms are uploaded next to it.
- `DeviceRun::forward` is split. `forward` embeds the token IDs into `x`,
  then `forward_body` runs the layers, the final norm and the logits over
  the rows already in `x`. `DeviceDraft` writes the head's input into `x`
  itself: the main embedding row (widened from BF16), the two RMSNorms into
  the halves of a joined row in the order [embedding, hidden] (as on the
  CPU), then `fc`.
- Several true pairs go through the head as one multi-row forward (prompt
  chunks of 128 rows; the m + 1 pairs of a verify pass). Each draft of the
  chain is a 1-row forward. The chain's head positions are taken back with
  `DeviceRun::rewind`, which only works for runs without DeltaNet layers
  (KV rows past the position are overwritten later).
- The draft g is row 0 of the head's `norm` buffer (the last row through
  the final norm), read after the logits. Its draft is the argmax of the N
  subset logits on the host, with ties going to the smaller ID.
- The `Draft` trait (`absorb` the true pairs, `draft` k tokens) has two
  implementations: `CpuDraft` (ADR 0027) and `DeviceDraft`.
  `speculative::generate` takes either one. `<engine>-speculative
  --draft-on device|cpu`, where the device is the default and needs a subset.
- `device_real_speculative`: the generated IDs equal plain greedy with the
  CPU draft (full vocabulary and a 4096-token subset) and with the device
  draft, at depths 1 and 3. The device drafts equal the CPU drafts from the
  prompt's last pair, and drafting again after the rewind gives the same
  drafts.

## Consequences

M5, the same 8 prompts and settings as ADR 0027 (851 decoded tokens, decode
phase only). The IDs equal plain greedy in all 12 runs.

(The plain rates of 69.5-69.9 tok/s in two runs are machine noise; the
speedup compares runs made back to back.)

- The best setting is still 8,192 rows and 2 drafts: 1.54x (CPU draft:
  1.40x). The acceptance is the same as with the CPU draft (60.5 / 31.6 %),
  so the drafts agree.
- A verify pass now costs 1.04-1.12 decode steps (CPU draft: 1.12-1.22).
  The GPU no longer idles between passes.
- One head forward costs about 1 ms. The memory bandwidth limit for 41 MB +
  16.8 MB would be about 0.5 ms. Timed with a sync after each stage
  (temporary patch), the median 1-row forward was 0.19 ms for the input
  (embedding row and two norms), 0.26 ms for `fc` (4 MB) and 0.76 ms for the
  block, final norm and projection. Most of the gap is fixed dispatch and
  sync cost: one sync per forward, since the next draft needs the argmax.
- A depth of 3 is slower than 2: the third draft is accepted only 16 % of
  the time and costs a forward (~1.1 ms) plus a longer verify.
- The head over the prompt takes 2.4-3.6 ms for these prompts (CPU:
  16-27 ms).

Remaining levers, each a few percent: the argmax of the verify rows on the
GPU (~0.4 ms per pass on the CPU), fewer dispatches and syncs per head
forward, and better acceptance at the start of a pass. ROCm needs the
snapshots of ADR 0026 first.
