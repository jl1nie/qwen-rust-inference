The water cycle is the continuous, circular process that moves water around the Earth. It works like a giant, invisible conveyor belt that keeps our planet in balance.

Here is the simple breakdown of the cycle:

1.  **Evaporation (The Start):** The Sun heats up oceans, lakes, and rivers. This heat turns liquid water into **water vapor** (water gas) and turns it into **steam**.
2.  **Transpiration (The Leaves):** Plants release water vapor through their leaves, adding more water to the air.
3.  **Condensation (The Clouds):** As the water vapor rises and cools, it turns back into tiny liquid droplets, forming **clouds**.
4.  **Precipitation (The Drop):** When the clouds get too heavy, they release water in the form of rain, snow, sleet, or hail.
5.  **Runoff (The Flow):** This water falls off the ground and flows into rivers, lakes, and oceans.
6.  **Collection (The Return):** These rivers and lakes eventually flow back into the oceans, where they mix with the fresh water from evaporation, completing the loop.

**In short:** Water goes up, turns into steam, forms clouds, falls as rain, and flows back down to start the cycle again.<|im_end|>

void reverse_string(char *s) {
    int len = strlen(s);
    if (len == 0) return;

    // Use two pointers to start and end

    int right = len - 1;

    while (left < right) {

        // Move pointers
        left++;
        right--;
    }
}

Here is a step-by-step explanation of how branches work and how to merge them.

### 1. How a Branch Works

A branch is essentially a copy of your current working directory. When you create a new branch, Git creates a new directory structure that mirrors your current state, but it does not contain your actual working files.

**The Workflow:**
1.  **Create a Branch**: You create a new branch (e.g., `feature/new-feature`) from your main branch.
2.  **Clone the Branch**: You clone the branch into your local repository. This creates a separate repository with the same code as your main branch but without your working files.
3.  **Work on the Branch**: You can now make changes, commit them, and push to your local repository.

円周率（π）は、**「円が何回も巻くか」**というイメージで説明すると、小学生でも理解しやすいです。

### 1. 円と弧のイメージ
円を 360 度（1 圈）に切ったとき、その弧の長さと円の半径（R）の関係は、以下の式で表されます。

$$ \text{弧の長} = \text{半径} \times \text{弧の度数} $$

ここで、弧の度数（$n$）は、円周率 $\pi$ を使って計算できます。
つまり、弧の長を $L$ とすると、
$$ L = \pi \times n $$

### 2. 具体的な例：1 圈の弧
円を 1 圈（360 度）に切ったとき、その弧の長さは $360$ です。
これを式に代入すると：
$$ 360 = \pi \times 360 $$

ここで、$360$ は「1 圈の度数」なので、$\pi$ は「1 圈の弧の長さ」を表します。
つまり、**1 圈の弧の長さは、半径の 360 倍の $\pi$ です。**

### 3. 実際の計算例
例えば、半径 $R = 5$ の円を 1 圈に切ったとき、その弧の長さは：
$$ L = 5 \times \pi = 5\pi $$

これを小数で計算すると：
$$ 5 \times 3.14159... \approx 15.71 $$
